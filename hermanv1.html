How do I open a company in Bulgaria?

We handle the whole process for you: reserving the company name, preparing the articles of association, opening the bank account, registering in the Commercial Register and arranging the tax and social security registrations. What is the difference between an EOOD and an OOD?

An EOOD is a limited liability company with a single owner, an OOD has two or more partners. Both have a symbolic minimum capital and both limit your liability to what you invest. They are the two most popular forms for foreign entrepreneurs and we advise you which one fits your plans.

How is a Bulgarian company taxed?

Corporate profit tax is 10 percent, dividends are taxed at 5 percent and personal income tax is a flat 10 percent. The standard VAT rate is 20 percent. Bulgaria also offers specific regimes for small businesses, so we check what applies to your case.

You must register when your taxable turnover passes the statutory threshold, when you trade goods within the European Union or when you import goods. Voluntary registration is possible as well. We follow your turnover and warn you in time.

It depends on the customer and their location. For B2B sales to an EU business with a valid VAT number, reverse charge generally applies. VIES is the EU system used to check VAT identification numbers for cross-border trade. Businesses commonly use it to verify an EU customer’s VAT number before applying the relevant B2B VAT treatment. EOOD and OOD Bulgaria, difference and complete guide to get started from a trusted accountant

EOOD or OOD: the two company forms that almost every foreign entrepreneur in Bulgaria chooses. Here is how they differ and which one fits your project.

An EOOD is a company with a single owner whose liability is limited to the capital contributed. It is the simplest and cheapest way for one person to start a business in Bulgaria. You can be the owner and the manager at the same time, even if you do not live in Bulgaria.

An OOD is the same kind of limited liability company, but with two or more partners. The partners can be companies as well as individuals, and the articles of association determine who manages the company and how the profit is distributed. An OOD also keeps the door open to convert into an AD if you later want to bring in investors.

Both forms are registered in the Bulgarian Commercial Register through the Registry Agency. You need a registered address in Bulgaria, a manager who can be of any nationality, and a corporate bank account. The registration itself is fast; preparing the documents correctly is where most of the time goes.

  • EOOD: one owner, symbolic minimum capital, fastest to set up
  • OOD: two or more partners, flexible profit sharing
  • AD: for larger structures that want to issue shares
  • Branch office or sole trader: for existing foreign companies and freelancers

Amazon FBA VAT: guide for Bulgarian companies

Selling through Amazon or another marketplace from Bulgaria? Then VAT is your biggest compliance risk. This is how to keep it under control.

As soon as you store goods in a Bulgarian warehouse you hold stock in Bulgaria, and that normally makes you liable for Bulgarian VAT registration. The same applies when you buy or sell goods within the European Union, or when your taxable turnover passes the statutory threshold.

For business to consumer sales in other EU countries you can use the One Stop Shop: one return and one payment in the country where you are registered, instead of registering in every country where you sell. For distance sales to consumers in your own country the local rules apply.

Marketplaces such as Amazon are often treated as the deemed supplier for imports and for certain consumer sales. That shifts part of the VAT obligation to the platform, but it does not remove your duty to file returns, keep records and report intra community transactions.

  • Bulgarian VAT registration and deregistration
  • Monthly VAT returns and EU sales lists
  • OSS and IOSS declarations
  • Advice before you put stock in a Bulgarian warehouse

VASP license in Bulgaria: Reporting Duties

Crypto activities in Bulgaria are regulated and the reporting duties are strict. Here is what a service provider has to arrange.

Companies that exchange crypto for fiat currency, operate a trading platform or hold crypto on behalf of clients need a VASP licence and must comply with anti money laundering rules: client identification, transaction monitoring and reporting of unusual activity.

On top of the licensing regime, European rules such as MiCA set requirements on information, capital and governance. Bulgarian companies must also report their crypto holdings and transactions correctly in their accounts and in their tax returns.

Reporting duties go further than tax. You must be able to show who your clients are, where the funds come from and how you valued your holdings at the balance sheet date. We set up the records, the valuation method and the internal procedures together with you.

  • VASP licence support and ongoing compliance
  • Valuation of crypto assets in your accounts
  • AML procedures and client files
  • Tax reporting for companies and individuals

Social security in Bulgaria: employees, managers and self-employed

Hiring yourself or your first employee in Bulgaria? Then social security is the part that surprises most foreign entrepreneurs. This is how the system is built.

Everyone who works in Bulgaria is in principle covered by the state social security system. An employment contract means the employer registers the employee before the first working day, withholds the contributions from the salary and pays them to the revenue office together with the employer share.

A manager or owner who works for the company can also be insured, even without an employment contract, because the position itself can create an insurance obligation. Self-employed people register their activity, declare their income and pay contributions on the base that the law sets for them.

The contribution base has a statutory minimum and maximum, and the rates are split between employer and employee. Health insurance, pension and the additional mandatory pension contributions are calculated on that base, so a small change in salary has an effect on the total cost.

  • Registration of employees before the first working day
  • Monthly payroll, contributions and payslips
  • Insured status for managers and owners
  • Self-employed registration and annual declarations

Tax residency in Bulgaria: what the 183-day rule really means

The 183-day rule is the best-known test for Bulgarian tax residency, but it is not the only one. Here is how to look at your own situation.

Someone who is present in Bulgaria for more than 183 days in any twelve-month period is generally treated as a Bulgarian tax resident. Bulgaria also applies other criteria, such as having a permanent home, habitual residence or the centre of vital interests in the country.

If you are a tax resident of Bulgaria, you are in principle taxed here on your worldwide income. If you are not, you are taxed only on your Bulgarian source income, such as income from a Bulgarian company or Bulgarian property.

Two countries can claim you at the same time. Most cases are then settled by the tie-breaker rules in the tax treaty between Bulgaria and your other country, which usually look at your permanent home and your personal and economic ties first.

  • Day counting and evidence of presence
  • Centre of vital interests and permanent home
  • Tax treaty tie-breaker rules
  • Practical consequences for your salary and dividends

UBO register in Bulgaria: who has to be reported?

Every Bulgarian company has to know who ultimately stands behind it, and in most cases it has to report that person to the register of beneficial owners. Here is what that means in practice.

The ultimate beneficial owner is the natural person who ultimately owns or controls the company. As a rule of thumb it is the person who holds more than 25 percent of the shares or voting rights, or who exercises control in another way.

If no natural person meets that test, the company has to look further up the chain, for example to the individuals who control a parent company abroad. If that also leads nowhere, the company reports the person who holds the highest position in management.

The information has to be registered, and it has to stay accurate. Changes in ownership, in management or in the way control is exercised have to be filed, and the register can impose fines when the data is missing or out of date.

  • Mapping the ownership and control chain
  • Preparing and filing the UBO declaration

Annual obligations of a Bulgarian company: the calendar to follow

A Bulgarian company does not only file throughout the year. There is a fixed set of annual tasks, with their own deadlines. This is the calendar we keep for our clients.

During the year the company keeps its accounting records, files its monthly or quarterly VAT returns and pays its taxes. At year end the bookkeeping has to be closed and the financial statements are prepared from it.

The annual financial statements are adopted by the general meeting of the owners and then filed with the Commercial Register, within the legal deadline. Depending on the size of the company, an audit may also be required before the statements are approved.

On top of that come the annual tax return and the annual declaration of the taxes withheld during the year, and for companies with staff the annual summaries for each employee. Missing a deadline usually means a fine rather than a warning.

  • Year-end closing and annual financial statements
  • General meeting and filing with the Commercial Register
  • Annual corporate tax return
  • Annual payroll and withholding summaries

Starting a company in Bulgaria, step by step

From the first document to a fully operational company: this is the order in which the steps follow, and how long each of them usually takes.

It starts with the choice of the legal form and the name of the company. We check whether the name is still available, reserve it and prepare the articles of association, which describe the owner, the manager, the activity and the capital.

Then the capital is deposited in a temporary bank account in Bulgaria and the file is submitted to the Commercial Register. Once the company is registered, that account becomes the corporate account and the company can start invoicing.

Finally come the registrations with the revenue office, the social security and, where needed, for VAT. You do not have to be in Bulgaria for any of this: with a power of attorney signed abroad we handle the steps in Sofia.

  • Choice of form, name reservation and articles of association
  • Capital, bank account and registration in the Commercial Register
  • Tax, VAT and social security registrations
  • Realistic timing: a few working days for the register, one to two weeks for the rest
